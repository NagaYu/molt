\noindent\textbf{Where the hypothesis fails, stated plainly.}
The design hypothesis had two parts.
First, carrying the KV cache across the switch would let the small model continue \emph{the same sentence} better than discarding or re-reading the context.
Second, molt's additions would make the simple method of~\citep{heo2026crossmodel} sufficient where it alone is not.
For 1.5B$\to$0.5B, it does not.
The 3B$\to$1.5B pair is \pending{still running}.

\paragraph{The transferred cache does not carry the content.}
With a mapped cache (C or D), the receiver keeps the topic and the voice of the text but loses its specifics.
Needle accuracy is \res{q.15b-05b.C.needle} for C and \res{q.15b-05b.D.needle} for D, against \res{q.15b-05b.A2.needle} when the small model re-reads the text.
On GSM8K the scores are \res{q.15b-05b.C.gsm8k} and \res{q.15b-05b.D.gsm8k}, against \res{q.15b-05b.A2.gsm8k}.
molt's anchors make the seam smaller than C's.
Paired agreement is $+$\res{qp.15b-05b.D.C.agree_post}, CI \res{qp.15b-05b.D.C.agree_post.ci}, and the seam is \res{qp.15b-05b.D.C.jump}~nats, CI \res{qp.15b-05b.D.C.jump.ci}.
Task score does not move: \res{qp.15b-05b.D.C.task}, CI \res{qp.15b-05b.D.C.task.ci}.
``Better than C'' and ``good enough'' are different claims, and only the first one holds here.
The teacher-forced gap of about a tenth of a nat per token (\cref{sec:results-intrinsic}) understates this badly: it compounds once the receiver generates freely.

\paragraph{Re-prefill is the better switch in exactly the setting we claim.}
A$'$ meets all three conditions of our scope.
It switches mid-decode with no retracted tokens, it is triggered by headroom, and it releases the sender before loading the receiver.
It survives every cell in which C is killed.
In our runs it resumes faster than D (\res{m.15b-05b.h345.A2.resume}\,s against \res{m.15b-05b.h345.D.resume}\,s at $h=345$) and uses less CPU energy (\res{m.15b-05b.h345.A2.ecpu}\,J against \res{m.15b-05b.h345.D.ecpu}\,J).
The mapping is cheaper only in compute and only with longer context: \res{r.15b-05b.D.P4096}\,s against \res{r.15b-05b.A2.P4096}\,s at 4\,096 tokens.
Our quality runs switched at \res{q.15b-05b.ctx.open}--\res{q.15b-05b.ctx.needle} tokens of context (medians per task), and the intrinsic gap grows with context (\res{int.15b-05b.D.P768} at 768 tokens).
So a long-context advantage for the transferred cache is \emph{untested}, and on the trend we measured, unlikely.

\paragraph{The careful release is a fix for one allocator setting, not a general requirement.}
Under MLX's default buffer cache, drop-then-load is killed in every cell, and the plain \texttt{clear\_cache()} variant survives erratically.
There, synchronise-clear-wait is what makes the ordered schedule survive.
Under the 20\,MiB cache limit that mlx-swift's examples set on iOS, both naive variants survive every cell we ran.
What holds under both settings is only the \emph{order}: the receiver must not be loaded before the sender is released.
That is a necessary condition, not a technique.

\paragraph{Nothing of molt v0.1's mapper survives.}
Its depth-linear layer pairing is far worse than the prior method's $R^2$-ranked top-$k$.
Held-out NLL is \res{fit.15b-05b.depth_nll} against \res{fit.15b-05b.kstar_nll}; needle accuracy is \res{q.15b-05b.Ddepth.needle}.

\paragraph{What does hold.}
Below the headroom a co-resident switch needs (\res{m.15b-05b.need}\,MiB here), the prefill-hand-off shape is killed.
A release-first switch completes with no retracted token, under both allocator settings.
At the context lengths we measured, the best thing to give the receiver in that situation is the transcript, not the transferred KV.
